# Ethics

CleanTrace exists for self-assessment and consent-based privacy cleanup.

Acceptable use:

- Investigating your own public exposure.
- Helping someone who has clearly asked you to review their exposure.
- Generating removal requests and tracking cleanup.
- Understanding risk from public identifiers and linked accounts.
- Checking explicit user-provided public onion URLs for your own identifiers without crawling.
- Importing lawful evidence you already have so it can be tracked and redacted locally.

Unacceptable use:

- Doxxing, stalking, harassment, impersonation, or intimidation.
- Searching arbitrary people without consent.
- Credential attacks, login attempts, CAPTCHA bypass, or password reset abuse.
- Scraping leaked databases or storing raw breach data.
- Importing leaked datasets, credential dumps, private keys, tokens, or stolen records.
- Crawling dark-web marketplaces, stolen-data forums, or illegal content.
- Bypassing website protections, paywalls, robots.txt, login walls, or anti-bot controls.
- Employment screening, intimidation, or revealing hidden/private data about another person.
- Uploading private data without explicit consent.

CleanTrace findings are confidence-scored leads for manual review. They are not proof that an account belongs to a person.

Breach intelligence is metadata-only. CleanTrace should never be used to reconstruct stolen datasets
or display credentials. Tor checks are for user-provided public pages only; they are not a dark-web
search engine.
